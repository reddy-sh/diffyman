# Typesetting glossary

Anchor:
  A fixed point in a document that a link can jump to.
  Anchors are usually headings or explicitly named elements.

Baseline:
  The invisible line that most letters sit on.
  Descenders such as g and y drop below the baseline.

Caption:
  Short text that describes a figure, table or photo.
  Captions sit directly above or below the item they describe.

Drop cap:
  A large first letter that spans several lines of a paragraph.
  Drop caps are common in books and long magazine features.

Em dash:
  A dash about as wide as the letter M, used to set off a phrase.
  Some style guides put thin spaces on either side of it.

Footnote:
  A note at the bottom of a page, linked by a superscript number.
  Endnotes collect the same notes at the end of a chapter instead.

Gutter:
  The inner margin where two facing pages meet at the binding.
  A wide gutter keeps text from disappearing into the spine.

Hanging indent:
  A paragraph whose first line starts further left than the rest.
  Bibliographies and reference lists often use hanging indents.

Italic:
  A slanted style of a typeface used for emphasis and titles.
  True italics are drawn separately rather than simply slanted.

Justified text:
  Text aligned to both the left and the right margin.
  Justification needs good hyphenation to avoid wide word gaps.
